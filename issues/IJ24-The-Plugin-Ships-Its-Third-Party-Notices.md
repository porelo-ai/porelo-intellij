**What must be true.** Whoever installs the plugin finds, inside the plugin's zip, the full license and notice texts of
every third-party jar it bundles, in one `THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES` file made by the generator every Porelo package uses.

## How it stands

- The plugin ships as a zip, not as a `.deb` or `.rpm`. Its `lib/` holds three jars, read from the zip `buildPlugin`
  makes and from `dependencies --configuration runtimeClasspath`:
  - `porelo-intellij-<version>.jar`, the plugin itself.
  - `porelo-wire-0.5.0.jar`, Porelo's own client, `GPL-3.0-or-later` by its pom; the jar holds no license text.
  - `jspecify-1.0.0.jar`, `Apache-2.0`, pulled in by `porelo-wire`; the jar holds no `LICENSE` or `NOTICE`, only the
    license's URL in its manifest. Apache-2.0 asks a redistribution to give a copy of the license, which the zip does
    not.
- The shared generator, in `porelo-buildtools`, collects every bundled jar's `LICENSE` and `NOTICE` and the licenses
  they embed into one `THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES` file with the full texts. Nothing is built here before it is on `main` there
  and published.

## Acceptance

- The zip `buildPlugin` makes holds `THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES` with the full Apache-2.0 text for jspecify and the license
  of every other bundled jar, and a test fails when a bundled jar has no entry in it.
- The file is generated by the build, never edited by hand, and the build fails when the generator cannot run.

## To be checked

- Where in the plugin's zip the IDE and the Marketplace expect such a file, beside `lib/` or inside the plugin's jar.
- Whether jspecify needs to be bundled at all: its annotations are not read at run time, so leaving it out of the zip
  would leave only Porelo's own code in it.
- How the generator is run from Gradle, as `porelo-release` is today.
